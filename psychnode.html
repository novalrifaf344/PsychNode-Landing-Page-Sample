Concept Glossary

Key Concepts Explored

Every term defined below comes directly from the script. Search and explore.

Sympathetic Nervous System

Your body's survival mode — it floods you with cortisol, raises heart rate, and narrows focus to deal with immediate threats. Essential for survival, but destructive when chronically active.

Joe Dispenza — Breaking the Habit of Being Yourself

Parasympathetic Nervous System

Your growth mode — calms the body, restores immune function, opens creative thinking, and enables future planning instead of reactive survival.

Joe Dispenza — Breaking the Habit of Being Yourself

Chronic Sympathetic Dominance

When modern pressures keep the stress alarm ringing nonstop, the body never receives the signal that danger has passed — locking you in survival mode indefinitely.

Joe Dispenza — Breaking the Habit of Being Yourself

Psychosomatic Effect

When chronic mental stress manifests as physical symptoms: persistent headaches, digestive problems, and insomnia. The body is responding accurately to a mind that believes it is always in danger.

Joe Dispenza — Breaking the Habit of Being Yourself

Amygdala

The brain's threat detection center. Under chronic stress, it grows larger and more reactive, making you biologically primed for fear over rational decision-making.

Robert Sapolsky — Behave

Prefrontal Cortex

The brain region responsible for planning, decision-making, and building the life you want. Chronic stress physically shrinks this area, reducing your capacity for growth.

Robert Sapolsky — Behave

The Simulation

Deepak Chopra's term for the layer of labels, fears, traumas, and conditioning that sits over who you really are. You mistake this constructed identity for reality — but a story can be rewritten.

Deepak Chopra — Metahuman

Neuroplasticity

The brain's ability to rewire itself based on what you believe and practice. Your identity is not fixed — it is a living, changeable architecture that responds to repeated thought and action.

Deepak Chopra — Metahuman

Body as Unconscious Mind

Dispenza's insight that the body stores and reinforces the mind's beliefs at a chemical level. When your story says "I am under threat," your nervous system agrees and your brain physically reshapes itself.

Joe Dispenza — Breaking the Habit of Being Yourself

Impostor Syndrome

The persistent feeling that your accomplishments are undeserved, that someone will "find you out." Up to 70% of people experience it — dismissing real evidence of competence as luck or timing.

Pauline Clance & Suzanne Imes (1978)

Dunning-Kruger Effect

A cognitive bias where people with limited knowledge dramatically overestimate their competence, while genuinely skilled people underestimate themselves — because the more they learn, the more they see how much more there is.

David Dunning & Justin Kruger (1999)

Competency Audit

A deliberate inventory of what you have actually completed, what measurable results you have produced, and what trusted people have told you — grounding confidence in evidence rather than feelings.

Synthesized from script framework

Identity-Based Habits

James Clear's principle that every action is a vote for the type of person you want to become. You don't rise to the level of goals — you fall to the level of your systems.

James Clear — Atomic Habits

Habit Stacking

Attaching new behaviors to existing routines: "After I pour my coffee, I write for ten minutes." The existing habit becomes a trigger; the new behavior rides on top of it until the chain runs automatically.

James Clear — Atomic Habits

Environment Design

Making constructive behavior easy and destructive behavior hard — because willpower runs out, but an intentionally designed environment works even on your worst day. That is not motivation. That is architecture.

James Clear — Atomic Habits

Social Influence on Habits

We absorb the habits of three groups: the close, the many, and the powerful. Social rejection activates the same brain regions as physical pain, making your social environment a biological force on behavior.

James Clear & Robert Sapolsky
